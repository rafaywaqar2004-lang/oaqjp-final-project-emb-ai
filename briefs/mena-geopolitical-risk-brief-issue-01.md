## Regional Snapshot

Of the 20 MENA economies the MENASA Risk Monitor tracks, **18 (90%) are directly named as affected
territory in at least one of the tool's 13 tracked live conflicts** -- only Tunisia and Turkey are not
tagged as directly affected by name in the current conflict dataset, though Turkey's proximity to the Syria
transition and its own role as a mediator elsewhere in the region means "not directly affected" undersells
its actual exposure; that's a scope limitation of the underlying dataset, not a claim that Turkey is
insulated. The regional average composite risk score across all 20 MENA states is **40.9/100** (Moderate
Risk), with a wide spread: Yemen sits highest at 71.3 (Higher Risk), the UAE lowest at 15.6 (Lower Risk) --
a 55.7-point gap that says more about the region's actual heterogeneity than any single average number
could. Five states moved more than 7 points on the composite score in the past year; all five are covered
below or in "Also Watching."

## Conflict Spotlight: The 2026 Iran-Israel-US War

**Status: active, unresolved.** What began as a 12-day conflict in June 2025 -- Israeli strikes on 900+
Iranian targets, a US strike on the Fordow, Natanz, and Isfahan nuclear sites, and a fragile June 24
ceasefire -- reignited fully on February 28, 2026 ("Operation Epic Fury"), with roughly 900 US-Israeli
strikes in 12 hours. Iran's Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei, was killed the same day and succeeded by Mojtaba
Khamenei on March 8. Estimated total deaths across all sides by late August 2026 range widely, from roughly
9,000 to over 18,000 -- source disagreement this large is itself informative about how little verified
information is reaching outside reporting.

**Economic impact:** Brent crude, trading near $70 before the war, spiked in stages -- to $80-82 in early
March, past $109 amid a Strait of Hormuz closure, and reportedly above $120-140 during subsequent
escalations, partially retracing on each ceasefire announcement. The IMF cut its 2026 global growth
forecast from 3.1% to 3.0% in July, citing the war's energy-shock effects, and CFR/WTO analysis has floated
a full-scale resumption costing the global economy as much as $2.2 trillion. Nine countries are named as
directly affected: Iran, Israel, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, and Iraq -- the last five
of those as targets of Iranian retaliatory strikes on Gulf soil, not combatants.

**Mediation:** Oman leads Hormuz safe-passage talks; Qatar hosts broader negotiations; US envoys Steve
Witkoff and Jared Kushner are named parties to the diplomatic track. No ceasefire has held for longer than
a partial retracing of oil prices before the next escalation.

## Conflict Spotlight: Red Sea Shipping Crisis & Houthi-Saudi Blockade

**Status: active, re-escalating.** The crisis traces to November 19, 2023 (the Houthi seizure of the cargo
ship *Galaxy Leader*) and has since expanded to over 100 targeted merchant vessels, four sunk, one seized,
at least eight seafarers killed. A pause held from roughly November 2025 (following the October 2025 Gaza
ceasefire) through late February 2026, before the Houthis renewed a ban on Israeli-linked shipping in June
2026 as the Iran war reignited -- and escalated further in July 2026 with a declared blockade specifically
targeting Saudi Arabia.

**Economic impact:** Suez Canal revenue collapsed from a record $9.4bn in 2023 to $3.9bn in 2024 (a ~58%
drop) as traffic diverted around the Cape of Good Hope, partially recovering to $4.67bn in FY2025/26 during
the pause -- before the July 2026 blockade threatened to reverse that recovery. War-risk marine insurance
premiums roughly doubled (from ~0.3% to ~0.75% of vessel value) following the blockade declaration, and
freight rates on affected routes have risen 25-40%. Six countries are named as directly affected: Yemen,
Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, and Israel.

**Worth watching alongside the Iran war above:** these are formally two separate conflicts in the
underlying dataset, but they are not independent of each other -- the Houthis' late-2025 pause and June
2026 resumption both tracked the Iran-Israel-US war's own escalation cycle. Treating them as one
combined regional shock, not two unrelated line items, is the more accurate read for anyone assessing
Gulf-adjacent shipping and energy risk this quarter.

## Conflict Spotlight: Syria's Post-Assad Transition

**Status: fragile but consolidating -- the one broadly improving story in this issue.** The Assad regime
fell December 8, 2024 after an 11-day rebel offensive led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, ending 13 years of
civil war. Ahmed al-Sharaa was declared transitional president in January 2025; an interim constitution
(March 2025) sets a 5-year path to elections. The US lifted its terrorism designation on the transitional
authorities in August 2026 -- a significant normalization signal even as the transition remains genuinely
contested (no single national armed force yet exists, and localized violence resumed in March 2026).

**Why this is the counter-narrative to watch:** Syria's own risk score rose sharply (+12.9 YoY, now the
region's second-highest at 67.4) -- the composite score is capturing real, ongoing instability. But the
World Bank's $216bn reconstruction estimate (a range of $140-345bn, which the Bank itself calls a likely
underestimate) sits alongside $28bn in investment commitments already attracted in 2025, including $14bn in
MOUs signed at a single August 2025 Damascus ceremony. Investors are moving faster than the risk score or
the sanctions picture alone would suggest -- a real divergence between backward-looking composite risk and
forward-looking capital allocation, worth tracking as a test case for how quickly a post-conflict transition
can attract investment ahead of, rather than after, measurable stabilization.

## Also Watching

Shorter items from this month's 13 tracked conflicts not given full spotlight treatment above:

- **Gaza War Aftermath:** ceasefire holding but repeatedly violated -- the most fragile of the "resolved"
  conflicts in the dataset, not a closed case.
- **Israel-Hezbollah / Lebanon Front:** ceasefire re-established April 2026, still fragile; Lebanon's own
  risk score (60.6) remains the region's third-highest despite a slight YoY improvement (-3.7).
- **Libya's Rival Governments Standoff:** frozen stalemate, no resolution in sight -- Libya's +7.5 YoY
  deterioration is a reminder that "frozen" conflicts still show up as rising risk when nothing improves.
- **Iran-Aligned Militia Attacks on US Forces in Iraq:** recurring, intensified during the 2026 Iran war --
  directly linked to the main spotlight conflict above, not an independent risk driver.
- **Egypt-Ethiopia Nile Dam (GERD) Dispute:** unresolved, no binding agreement despite the dam's physical
  completion -- a reminder that a completed infrastructure fact doesn't resolve the underlying political
  dispute.
- **Western Sahara Conflict & Algeria-Morocco Rupture:** low-intensity since the 2020 ceasefire collapse,
  with the diplomatic track shifting toward Morocco through 2025-2026.

*(The dataset's remaining conflicts -- the 2026 Pakistan-Afghanistan War, the India-Pakistan Kashmir
Crisis, and the Balochistan/CPEC Insurgency -- are South Asia-centered rather than MENA-centered and are
out of this issue's scope; they're better suited to a future South Asia-focused issue of this series, or
already have partial coverage in this project's separate sovereign-debt brief, which touches Pakistan's
domestic instability from a fiscal angle.)*

## Methodology and Scope

This issue's regional snapshot and risk-mover figures are computed directly from the MENASA Risk Monitor's
`scored_data.csv`; conflict detail, statistics, and sourcing are drawn from that tool's `LIVE_CONFLICTS`
dataset in `context_data.py`, itself sourced from Al Jazeera, Bloomberg, CFR, the Atlantic Council, the IMF,
the World Bank, and the UK House of Commons Library, among others -- full per-conflict source lists are
in the underlying dataset, not reproduced here for brevity. As with the other two briefs in this
project, every figure above traces to a specific cited row in that dataset; nothing here is estimated or
extrapolated beyond what the source data states.

**This is Issue No. 1 of a planned monthly series.** Each future issue will cover: an updated regional
snapshot and risk-mover table, 2-3 spotlighted conflicts or developments (prioritized by scale of change,
not a fixed rotation), a shorter "Also Watching" roundup of the rest, and an outlook section carried
forward from the prior issue's open questions -- Turkey's risk trajectory and Syria's investment-versus-risk
divergence are the two carried into Issue No. 2 above. Because the underlying MENASA Risk Monitor's
quantitative layer refreshes weekly via its own GitHub Actions pipeline but its qualitative conflict
narratives are a periodically curated snapshot (not a live feed), each issue's conflict-detail figures
should be treated as accurate as of this issue's stated month, not real-time.

This is a research/portfolio product, not a commissioned or institutional assessment, and should be read
accordingly.
